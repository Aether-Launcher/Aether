# Project Philosophy

## Keep the launcher focused
Aether's main job is to launch Minecraft and manage its instances. Features that aren't part of that core experience should usually come from extensions, so people can add what they need without making the launcher feel crowded.

The base app should stay small and predictable. Think of it as a useful foundation: extensions can add capabilities, but Minecraft stays at the center.

## Design language
Aether should feel minimal, modern, quick, and at home on the desktop. The interface should be calm and polished without drawing attention away from the task at hand.

Avoid making it feel like a website, game dashboard, social feed, ad-supported launcher, or settings app. References such as Pandora Launcher, VS Code, Linear, Raycast, Windows 11, Cursor, and Notion can be useful, but Aether should have its own character.

## Performance goals
- Cold start: under 2 seconds.
- Navigation: immediate, without noticeable pauses.
- Animation: smooth at 60 FPS.
- Idle memory use: below 100 MB.

These are targets, not a reason to trade away reliability or accessibility.

## The intended feeling
When someone opens Aether, the interface should feel simple and get out of the way. It should not try to prove how many things it can do. The launcher supports the Minecraft experience; it isn't the focus of it.
